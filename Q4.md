# BUS5001 Q4 — Evaluating NotebookLM for Academic Research

## 1. Objective

This experiment evaluates an AI-powered notebook assistant for academic research and coursework. It investigates source-grounded question answering, academic briefing generation, quiz generation, and audio-based learning.

## 2. Tool and setup

- **Tool accessed:** Gemini Notebook interface at notebook.google.com
- **Notebook:** Q4 - Evaluating NotebookLM for Academic Research
- **Topic:** Comparing IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards and GRI Standards
- **Sources:** IFRS Foundation and Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) official webpages
- **Evaluation approach:** Review output relevance, verify claims against the source material, assess academic usefulness, and identify limitations.

## 4. Preliminary observations

### Test 1 — Source-grounded Q&A
The tool produced a structured explanation of sustainability reporting and compared the objectives of the IFRS and GRI standards. Source citations were displayed. Citation accuracy still needs to be checked against the original passages.

### Test 2 — Academic briefing
The tool generated an executive summary, sections on purpose and intended audience, a comparison table, and practical implications. It also identified information that the uploaded sources did not establish. Detailed claims require verification, particularly where source coverage is limited.

### Test 3 — Quiz generation
Results will be recorded after reviewing the generated questions and answers.

### Test 4 — Audio Overview
Results will be recorded after generating and listening to the audio.

## 5. Evaluation criteria

- **Accuracy and relevance:** Are claims correct, relevant, and supported by the sources?
- **Academic usefulness:** Does the feature improve understanding, revision, or research efficiency?
- **Limitations:** Does it omit context, oversimplify concepts, rely on incomplete evidence, or encourage over-reliance on AI?

## 6. Limitations

This is a small exploratory experiment using two sources and a limited number of tests. The findings cannot establish general performance across subjects or academic tasks. Outputs must be checked against the original sources, and the exact product name and features used should be confirmed.
